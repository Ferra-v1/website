The arm is the loan. Each dot that turns solid has been paid back.

1

A company asks.

An example company rents robots to factories and wants 50 more, at $125,000 each. It asks Ferra what it could borrow.

2

Ferra prices them.

We would check what sellers ask and what auctions pay. These 50 are new, so their price is what they cost: $6,250,000.

3

Ferra works out the loan.

We would suggest a loan of 70%: $4,375,000. The company would pay the other $1,875,000 itself.

4

A lender decides.

We plan to take the numbers to lenders, and each says yes or no. Ferra has not arranged a loan yet.

5

The lender would pay.

The company would get $4,375,000 and buy the robots. It would pay Ferra a one-time fee of about $65,625.

6

The robots earn rent.

Customers would pay the company rent: in our example $4,500 a robot, or $225,000 a month.

7

The company pays it back.

From that rent, it would pay the lender about $121,500 a month, plus interest. Each payment turns more of the arm solid.

8

Nothing is owed.

After month 36 the company owns all 50 robots, and they keep earning rent.

What if a loan goes bad?

The lender can sell the robots, and a rushed sale may not cover what is owed.

How we work out these numbers
  • Example: 50 new arms at $125,000 each, with 70% lent, repaid in equal parts over 36 months. Real terms depend on the lender and the robots. Nothing here is an offer to lend.
  • The rent is our example, the same one the price tool opens on. The lender would set its own interest, so we show no rate here.
  • Prices come from 1,744 public listings on 7 marketplaces and 26 auction results. Listings show asking prices. Auction results show prices paid.
