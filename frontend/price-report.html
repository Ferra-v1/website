Every dot is a robot arm for sale.

433 arms with a price and a year, placed by asking price. The dark dots are 10 real sales at auction. Hover or tap any dot to see the record, with its link.

A dot's place shows its price only. Ages differ across a row, and older arms ask less. The asking prices are from 2026 and the auctions ran from 2018 to 2026 with no adjustment for inflation, so older sales read low.

The last seven families share one rate: 4.13% a year, from all arms pooled together.

Listings counts every listing in a family. In the estimate counts the priced, dated listings behind its values.

Typical is the middle asking price we estimate. Low and high are the 10th and 90th percentile: 8 in 10 asking prices fall between them.

Loses a year is how fast asking prices fall with age. A family gets its own rate once it has at least 30 dated listings. Values at 5 and 10 years use the 4.13% a year pooled across all arms.

The prices come from two kinds of public record.

Listings show asking prices, and auctions show what buyers paid. In the 10 auction lots we could match to listings, the middle one brought 24% of the typical asking price: 50% for robots under 10 years old and 17% for older ones. These are few sales, and older sales read low.

Where the numbers come from

  • Example values by age follow our model for a fully installed robot: about 80% of the new price after one year, 60% after two, 44% after three. Robot arms listed for sale price the arm alone, so they sit lower. See how we work it out.
  • 1,744 public used-robot listings from 7 marketplaces and 26 auction results from 2018 to 2026, collected for Ferra's price tool. Listings show asking prices; auction results show prices paid.
