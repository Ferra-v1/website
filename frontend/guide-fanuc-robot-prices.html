Asking prices by series and age

FANUC sells its arms in series. The R-2000 is FANUC's big arm for heavy loads, common in car plants and palletizing, the M-series covers everything from small M-10 handling arms to the giant M-900, and the ARC Mate and LR Mate are the welding arms and the small bench-top arms. Here is what each typically asks, for the arm alone:

The range around each figure is wide. FANUC sellers list fewer arms with both a price and a build year than KUKA or ABB sellers do, so our FANUC prices lean partly on what all arms ask, and the band where 8 in 10 asking prices fall is broad. For a 10 year old R-2000 it runs from $10,300 to $41,500. Treat the typical figure as the middle of a range and check it against the listings on the R-2000 page, the M-series page and the ARC and LR Mate page.

The young R-2000iB arms sold near 70% of what sellers ask, while the arms of 14 years and older brought around a fifth. Across every arm we matched, arms under 10 brought a middle 50% and arms of 10 or older 17%. Auctions ran over several years and we do not adjust them for inflation, so the oldest sales read a little low.

Which FANUC models search for most

The R-2000iB is the arm people look up most, often with its version: the 165F and the 210F, which carry 165 and 210 kilograms. Our price tool prices the R-2000iB from the R-2000 series level, because too few R-2000iB listings show a build year for a separate adjustment. At 10 years old it reads $20,700 typical asking and an expected sale of $8,600.

FANUC against KUKA and ABB

At 10 years old, a FANUC M-series arm asks $17,600, against $14,700 for a KUKA KR arm and $14,900 for a mid-size ABB IRB arm. FANUC arms ask somewhat more at the same age. That fits their place in the market: FANUC arms are among the most common in factories, so more buyers already know how to program and maintain one. Because the FANUC figures rest on fewer dated listings, read that gap as a lean, and check the listings for your model.

How we worked it out

All three series lose asking price at the rate we measured across every arm, about 4.1% a year, because no FANUC series has enough dated listings to measure its own rate. Each series has its own price level from its own listings, pulled toward the all-arms level when it has few. The full method, including how we test it, is on how we price robots.

Before you buy a used FANUC

Match the arm to its controller: the R-30iA, R-30iB and older R-J3 cabinets run different software, and a missing or mismatched controller can cost more than the arm. Ask for the hours, the alarm history and a video of the arm running through its full range. If the arm cannot be seen working, the auction figures above are a fairer guide than the asking prices.

Questions people ask

How much is a used FANUC robot?

At 10 years old, an R-2000 typically asks $20,700, an M-series arm $17,600 and an ARC Mate or LR Mate $13,600, for the arm alone.

What is a used FANUC R-2000iB worth?

A 10 year old R-2000iB typically asks $20,700, and we estimate a planned private sale at about $8,600.

What do FANUC robots sell for at auction?

In the sales we matched, from about a fifth of the asking price for older arms to about 70% for young R-2000iB arms.

Why is the price range so wide for FANUC?

Fewer FANUC listings show both a price and a build year, so each series' price leans partly on what all arms ask.

Does a FANUC price include the controller?

Our figures are for the arm alone. A working controller that matches the arm adds value we do not count.
