What if a loan goes bad?

The robots are the collateral: what you can sell to get your money back. We aim to keep each loan smaller than what the robots could be sold for.

5.2%Expected loss, counting everything that can go wrong. Expected loss is what a lender should plan to lose on average, as a share of the loan.
0.3%Expected loss if no robot company fails.

In our example, the loan shrinks faster than the robots lose value.

50 robot arms worth $6,250,000. The advance rate, the share of the value that gets lent, is 70%. So the loan is $4,375,000, paid back in equal monthly parts over 36 months, with a fee of about $65,625 at close. The cushion is the value left above the loan.

Move across the chart to read any month. The values follow our risk model's curve for a fully installed robot, which is an assumption. Real terms depend on the lender and the robots.

Auctions pay far less than sellers ask.

24%

The middle price paid in the 10 auction lots we could match to listings, as a share of the typical asking price for the same robot at the same age. Listings are only asking prices: nobody has paid them yet.

Read this with care. These are few sales. The asking prices are from 2026 and the auctions ran from 2018 to 2026 with no adjustment for inflation, so older sales read low.

Our example lends 70% of what a fully installed robot is worth in our model. Listings and auctions price the arm alone, so the two do not compare directly. Set your own advance rate against these sales.

FerraExample terms

Equipment loan, robot fleet

Proposed terms for the example fleet. Capitalized words mean what they say here.

Collateral
50 industrial arms, each a complete palletizing cell, installed. The lender can sell these if the Loan is not paid back.
Fleet Value
$6,250,000, the price of each robot on the Price List, added up.
Advance Rate
70% of Fleet Value. This is the share of the value that gets lent.
Loan
$4,375,000, the Fleet Value times the Advance Rate.
Term
36 months from close.
Repayment
Equal monthly amounts of principal over the Term.
Fee
About 1.5% of the Loan at close: $65,625.
Arranger
Ferra, which prices the robots and arranges the Loan.
Lender
A private credit lender that finances equipment and provides the money.

Real terms depend on the lender and the robots. This is an example. It is not an offer or a promise to lend.

Where the numbers come from

  • Our example is 50 robot arms, each fully installed with its gripper and controller, at $125,000 each, or $6,250,000 in all. A loan of 70% is $4,375,000, with a fee of about 1.5% ($65,625) when the loan starts, paid back in equal monthly parts over 36 months. Real terms depend on the lender and the robots. Nothing on this site is an offer or commitment to lend.
  • Example values by age follow our model for a fully installed robot: about 80% of the new price after one year, 60% after two, 44% after three. Robot arms listed for sale price the arm alone, so they sit lower. See how we work it out.
  • From Ferra's study of 18 robot types (17 pages). The 0.3% leaves out losses caused by robot companies failing; the 5.2% includes every cause.
  • 1,744 public used-robot listings from 7 marketplaces and 26 auction results from 2018 to 2026, collected for Ferra's price tool. Listings show asking prices; auction results show prices paid.
