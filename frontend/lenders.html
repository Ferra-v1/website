How pricing works. Every robot valued on its own.

  • Used-robot listings

    Asking prices on online marketplaces. One marketplace alone lists 28,258 used industrial robots.3 We treat asking prices as an upper bound.

  • Auction results

    Prices paid when fleets and factories are sold off. They set the floor a lender can count on.

  • The robot's details

    Model, build year and condition, so each robot is priced against its own family and age.

Listings and auctions price the arm alone. The value of a complete installed cell comes from the risk engine's curve, an assumption we state openly.4

Notes

  1. Example fleet: 50 industrial arms, each a complete palletizing cell (arm, gripper and controller, installed) at $125,000, a fleet value of $6,250,000. A 70% advance gives a $4,375,000 loan, with a fee of about 1.5% at close ($65,625). Charts show the loan repaid in equal monthly amounts of principal over 36 months, the term the price tool uses for this cell. In the live fleet preview, the value line follows the risk engine's curve for the robot type you choose. Actual terms depend on the lender and the robots. Nothing on this site is an offer or commitment to lend.

  3. Count of used industrial robots listed for sale on one online marketplace.
  4. Resale values by year follow the risk engine's curve for a palletizing cell: 80% of the new installed price after one year, 60% after two, 44% after three. The same curve sets the expected loss figures and the price tool's values for robots up to 2 years old. Used-robot listings price the robot alone, so they sit lower. See the methodology.
