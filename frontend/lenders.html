How pricing works

Every robot in a fleet gets its own value, built from what that type of robot sells for used. The loan is sized from those values.

  1. 01
    Used-robot listings

    Asking prices on online marketplaces. One marketplace alone lists 28,258 used industrial robots3. We treat asking prices as an upper bound.

  2. 02
    Dealer sales

    What dealers and refurbishers actually sell used robots for.

  3. 03
    Auctions

    Closing prices when fleets and factories are sold off. These set the floor a lender can count on.

What a term sheet looks like

The example fleet as a lender would see it1. Real terms depend on the lender and the robots.

  1. CollateralEvery robot in the fleet, listed by type.
  2. Fleet valueThe sum of the Ferra price for each robot.
  3. Advance rateThe share of fleet value lent. 70% in our example, set below resale value so the robots cover the loan as they age.
  4. Loan amountFleet value times the advance rate.
  5. Fee at closeAbout 1.5% of the loan, paid once at close.

Where Ferra fits

Ferra sits between the robot company and the lender. It prices the fleet and arranges the loan; the lender provides the money.

Robot company

Owns the robots and rents them to its customers. It borrows against the fleet and repays from rental income.

Ferra

Prices every robot from used-robot listings, dealer sales and auctions, then arranges the loan. Ferra does not lend.

Private credit

Funds that finance equipment provide the money and hold the priced fleet as collateral.
