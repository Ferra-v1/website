How much is a used KUKA KR 210 worth?

A KUKA KR 210 built in 2016 typically lists for $13,800 for the arm alone, and most sellers ask between $8,200 and $23,200.

In a planned private sale, where the owner has time to find a buyer, it would likely bring about $5,800.

Two things stand out. The asking price falls slowly and steadily, about 4.1% a year, so a 20 year old KR 210 still asks $9,000, a bit over half of the $17,000 a 5 year old one asks. The expected sale falls much faster, and it drops in one step between the ninth and tenth birthdays.

Why the sale figure drops at 10 years old

An asking price is what a seller hopes to get. To learn what buyers actually pay, we matched auction results to listings of the same robot at the same age. Arms under 10 years old brought a middle 50% of what sellers ask for the same arm. Arms 10 years or older brought a middle 17%. Older robots are harder to sell: spare parts get scarcer, the controller may be a generation behind, and fewer integrators want to support it.

We treat that auction share as the floor, what a rushed sale would bring, and put the expected sale halfway between the asking price and the floor on a ratio scale. At 9 years old a KR 210 asks $14,400 and would likely sell for $10,200. At 10 it asks $13,800 and would likely sell for $5,800. Our price tool smooths that step over the year between the two birthdays on its chart, and prints the whole-year figures you see here.

How the KR 210 compares with other KUKA arms

At 10 years old the KR 210 asks a little less than the KUKA KR family as a whole ($14,700), close to the KR 240 ($13,600) and well under the smaller KR 16 ($16,900). That fits what the listings show: the big 200 kilogram class arms come out of car plants in large numbers, so the market has plenty of them, while small arms stay in demand for machine tending and light assembly.

Some KR 210 versions have enough listings for a price of their own:

  • KR 210 L150: typical asking $16,400 at 10 years old.
  • KR 210 L180: typical asking $9,700 at 10 years old.
  • KR 210 R3100: typical asking $16,600 at 10 years old.

Reach, mounting and the wrist all change what a buyer can do with the arm, and buyers pay for that.

How we worked it out

We collected public listings of used robot arms and kept the ones that show both a price and a build year. One model fitted across all of them says how asking prices fall with age, and each model gets its own adjustment from its own listings. The KR 210 has enough listings for its own adjustment, and it sits 6% below the KUKA KR family level. The band on the chart is where 8 in 10 asking prices for this model and age would fall.

We test the method by hiding each seller's listings and pricing them from everyone else's. Every step is written out on how we price robots, including the test results.

What to check before you buy one

The figures above are for an ordinary used arm. A specific KR 210 can be worth more or less for reasons a listing rarely shows. Ask for the build year from the plate on the arm, the operating hours, which controller comes with it and whether it boots without errors, and whether the teach pendant and the cables between arm and cabinet are included. Ask what it was used for: spot welding and heavy palletizing wear the gearboxes harder than handling light parts. If the seller cannot show it moving, price it nearer the auction floor ($2,400 at 10 years old) than the asking price.

Setting a KR 210 up costs extra and is a separate decision: a base, a gripper or weld gun, safety fencing and programming. Our price tool shows what a robot is worth fully set up when you enter what the whole cell cost.

Questions people ask

Is $13,800 what a used KR 210 sells for?

No. $13,800 is what sellers typically ask for a 10 year old KR 210. We estimate a planned private sale would bring about $5,800, and a rushed sale at auction about $2,400.

How fast does a KUKA KR 210 lose value?

Its asking price falls about 4.1% a year. What it would sell for falls faster after 10 years old, when buyers at auction pay a smaller share of the asking price.

Does the price include the controller?

Our figures are for the arm alone. Many listings include a controller, but we do not add anything for it, the gripper or the work to set the robot up.

Where do these prices come from?

From public listings of used robot arms and matched auction results, read with one model. The method is on how we price robots.

Can I price a KR 210 with a different build year?

Yes. Our price tool opens on the KR 210, and you can pick any year from 1995 to 2026.
