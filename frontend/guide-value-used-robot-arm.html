How to value a used robot arm before you buy

Work from the exact model and build year to the typical asking price, then down to what a sale would likely bring. For a KUKA KR 16 built in 2018, that is a typical asking price of $18,400 and an expected sale of $13,000.

The six steps below take about ten minutes with a listing in front of you.

Step 1: Name the exact model

"KUKA robot" is not enough. Prices differ a lot between models from one maker: at 8 years old a KUKA KR 16 typically asks $18,400, while the bigger KR 210 asks $15,000. Read the model from the plate on the arm or the seller's paperwork, and write down the version too (for example KR 16-2 or KR 210 R2700). If a listing does not name the model, ask before you do anything else.

Step 2: Read the build year from the plate

Age is the biggest single driver of a used arm's price. The plate on the arm carries the build year. A listing's year sometimes means the year the seller bought it or the year it was last serviced, and the controller can be younger or older than the arm. Each year of age takes about 4.1% off the typical asking price, and the tenth birthday matters more than the others, as step 5 shows.

Step 3: Find the typical asking price

With the model and year you can read the typical asking price, the middle of what sellers ask for that robot at that age. Our price tool does it in one step. For the KR 16 built in 2018 it reads $18,400, and most sellers ask between $10,800 and $31,300. If you cannot find the model at all, the all-arms average for that age is a fallback: $16,000 at 8 years old.

Step 4: Place this arm inside the range

The range is wide because arms differ. Move toward the high end for an arm that comes with its controller, teach pendant and cables, shows low hours, was used for light handling and can be seen running. Move toward the low end when parts are missing, the history is unknown, it worked in welding spatter, paint or a foundry, or the seller wants it gone this month.

Step 5: Work down to what it would likely sell for

Asking prices are not sale prices. When we matched auction results to listings of the same robot at the same age, arms under 10 years old sold for a middle 50% of the asking price, and arms 10 or older for 17%. That is the floor: what a rushed sale brings. A planned private sale lands between the floor and the asking price. We put it halfway on a ratio scale, which for this KR 16 is $13,000, with the floor at $9,200.

That expected sale is a fair first offer. A seller who has had the arm listed for months, or who is clearing a plant, will often take it.

Step 6: Price the set-up separately

A bare arm does nothing on its own. Budget separately for a base, the tool on its end, safety fencing, cables, programming and the time to get it running. Those costs do not change what the arm itself is worth, so keep them out of the offer. If you are valuing a robot that is already set up and working, our price tool shows that figure too, from what the whole cell cost.

Say a seller lists one at $31,300 with its controller, low hours and a video of it running. That is the top of the range, and the extras justify some of it. An offer near the typical asking price of $18,400 is reasonable, and the expected sale of $13,000 is where to start if the arm has no history.

Questions people ask

How do you value a used robot arm?

Find the exact model and build year, read the typical asking price for them, place the arm in the range by condition, then work down to a likely sale price and an auction floor.

What is a fair offer for a used robot arm?

The expected sale, halfway between the asking price and the auction floor on a ratio scale. For a KR 16 built in 2018 that is $13,000.

Does the age of a robot matter more than its hours?

Age moves the price most in our data, because every listing has an age and few show hours. Low hours still justify the high end of the range.

Should I pay more for a robot with its controller?

Yes, a matching working controller is worth paying for. Our figures are for the arm alone and leave it out.

Where can I check the price of my exact robot?

Our price tool prices any model we know for any build year.
