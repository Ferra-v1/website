A robot earns a little every month. You pay for it all on day one.

Say your robot costs $125,000 and rents for $4,500 a month, our example rent. It takes about 28 months of rent to earn its price back, before repairs and other costs.

So the more customers you win, the more money you need up front. A loan pays for the robots now, and the rent pays back the loan.

You could sell part of your company instead. With a loan you still own all of it. The lender can sell the robots if you stop paying.

Questions

Has Ferra arranged a loan yet?

Not yet. Ferra is new, and the loans on this site are examples.

Does Ferra lend the money?

No. Ferra prices the robots and plans to arrange the loan. Investment funds that lend money for machines would provide it. None has lent through us yet.

How much could I borrow?

In our example, 70% of what new robots cost. For a used robot, 70% of our estimate of what it is worth, set up and working. A real lender may count less and offer less.

What would it cost?

In our example, a fee of about 1.5% when the loan starts, plus the lender's interest.

How would I pay it back?

In our example, in equal monthly parts over 36 months. The rent your robots earn pays it.

Which robots could I borrow for?

The ones people buy and sell used, because that is how we can see what they are worth. We price robot arms from real listings today. Other robots come next. See the robots we price.

What if a loan goes bad?

A lender can sell the robots. A forced sale may not cover the loan, so we show lenders the sums. Read the rest on the lenders page.

Every loan puts more robots to work, and brings closer the day anyone can own a piece of them.
