You may have walked past one already.

Robots are starting to do real work in the real world. There will be many more.

Robots will do more and more of the world's work.

Over farms, down streets, inside warehouses and factories. Real work in real places, here on Earth.

This is where the world is going.

When a robot does the work, the money goes to whoever owns it.

Right now, most people have no way to own any of them.

A robot at work holds its place.

  • Find the building.
  • Bolt it to the floor.
  • Teach it the job.
  • Keep it running.

Anyone else who wants that work has to do all of it again.

We think that head start lasts. That is why these robots are worth owning.

Before anyone can own a robot, they need to know what it is worth.

So we start with a fair price.

$14,700is what sellers typically ask for a 10 year old robot arm.

$6,100is our estimate of what it would likely sell for.

Our rule changes at 10 years old: older arms brought far less at auction, so the estimate drops in one step.

We read what sellers ask and what auctions pay. We price robot arms today, and other robots come next.

Next, loans.

Lenders lend money for trucks and planes because they know what those sell for used. With a fair price, they could do the same for robots.

We plan to arrange those loans for the companies that buy robots. Lenders would provide the money. We have not arranged a loan yet.

One day, a piece for anyone.

Once people can trust the price, more people can put their money behind robots. One day we want anyone to be able to own a piece of the robots doing the world's work.

You cannot invest through Ferra today. This is where we want to go.
