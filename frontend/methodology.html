How we price robots

How we work out what a robot is worth, so a lender can lend against it. This is the method behind the price report and the values in our loan examples.

01Where prices come from

Listings. Asking prices for used robots on online marketplaces. One marketplace alone lists 28,258 used industrial robots. Nobody has paid these prices yet, and they run above what robots sell for, so we treat them as the top of the range.

Auction results. Prices paid at auction when fleets, factories or companies are sold off. We treat them as the bottom of the range.

The robot's details. Model, build year and condition, so each robot is priced against its own family and age.

The price tool runs on 1,744 used-robot listings and 26 auction results, with the model and its accuracy shown on the page.

02Auctions pay far less than sellers ask

We could match 10 auction lots with a stated build year to listings for the same robot at the same age. The middle one brought 24% of the typical asking price. Robots under 10 years old brought 50% (6 lots), and older ones 17% (4 lots).

These are few sales. The asking prices are from 2026 and the auctions ran from 2018 to 2026 with no adjustment for inflation, so older sales read low. Purple Wave prices include a buyer's fee of about 10%. BTM Industrial prices are the winning bid, before any fee.

03Which listings we use

Not every listing can be used. From the listings that show a price and a build year, we keep the arms, collaborative arms and palletizers we can identify. Then we take out lots of several robots, new or broken robots, prices outside $500 to $400,000, repeats, parts and whole cells. That leaves 369 listings from 74 sellers.

04How good the estimate is

We tested the estimate on asking prices it had not seen, holding back one group of sellers at a time. The typical miss was 25%. Using only a family's middle price, with no model or age, the miss was 29%. Asking prices across all arms fall about 4.13% a year, and the true rate could be anywhere from 1.6% to 6.6%.

05How robots are grouped

Robots are grouped by type (for example industrial arms, collaborative arms, mobile robots, pallet movers, delivery and cleaning robots), and within a type by manufacturer and model wherever enough listings exist. Our listings cover arms only so far, in 11 families. A family is one maker's line of similar arms. Each fleet, meaning a company's group of robots, is priced robot by robot.

06What the curve shows

Our examples use one curve to size a loan: the share of its price when new that a fully installed robot keeps at each age. It is our risk model's curve for a palletizing cell, meaning an arm set up to stack boxes on pallets. It is an assumption, and it falls faster than the 4.13% a year we see in asking prices for the arm alone. The price report shows it in dollars for each robot type.

07How values become a loan

We aim to size the loan below what the robots would resell for, so the collateral, what a lender can sell if the loan is not paid back, covers the debt as the robots age. In our example fleet, the advance, the share of the value that gets lent, is 70% of fleet value. The actual advance depends on the robot type, its resale history and the lender.

Listings and auctions price the arm alone. The value of a complete installed robot comes from our risk model's curve, an assumption we state openly.

08What the examples on this site are

The resale values on our marketing pages follow our risk model's curve for a palletizing cell. The price tool runs on real listings and auction results.

09The study

Our study covered 18 robot types and runs to 17 pages. In it, expected loss, what a lender should plan to lose on average, was 0.3% once robot company failure was removed, and 5.2% with all causes included. Lenders can ask us for the study summary through the lender form.

Where the numbers come from

  • Our example is 50 robot arms, each fully installed with its gripper and controller, at $125,000 each, or $6,250,000 in all. A loan of 70% is $4,375,000, with a fee of about 1.5% ($65,625) when the loan starts, paid back in equal monthly parts over 36 months. Real terms depend on the lender and the robots. Nothing on this site is an offer or commitment to lend.
  • Example values by age follow our model for a fully installed robot: about 80% of the new price after one year, 60% after two, 44% after three. Robot arms listed for sale price the arm alone, so they sit lower. See how we work it out.
  • From Ferra's study of 18 robot types (17 pages). The 0.3% leaves out losses caused by robot companies failing; the 5.2% includes every cause.
  • Count of used industrial robots listed for sale on one online marketplace.
  • 1,744 public used-robot listings from 7 marketplaces and 26 auction results from 2018 to 2026, collected for Ferra's price tool. Listings show asking prices; auction results show prices paid.
