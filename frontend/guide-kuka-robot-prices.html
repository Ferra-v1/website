Bigger is not dearer. The small KR 16 asks more than the KR 210 at the same age, because small arms suit far more jobs and the big car-plant arms come onto the used market in large batches when a line closes. The models in blue have their own page with every listing and the chart for that model.

Reading a KUKA model name, and price per kilogram

After the KR number, a KUKA name often carries the reach in millimetres (R2700 means 2,700 millimetres) or an L for a long-arm version that carries less weight further out, such as the KR 210 L150. Because the number is the load, you can compare models on what each kilogram of lifting costs. At 10 years old the KR 16 asks about $1,100 per kilogram it can carry, and the KR 210 about $100. If your job needs a heavy load, the big arms are the cheapest way to buy lifting power on the used market.

How fast KUKA arms lose value

KUKA KR asking prices fall about 4.1% a year. KUKA is one family where we can also measure its own rate from its own listings, and it comes out at about 4.1% a year, close to the all-arms rate, so we use the all-arms rate for every family. What an arm would sell for falls faster after 10 years old: arms that old brought a middle 17% of the asking price at auction, against 50% for younger arms.

How we worked it out

One model fitted across every used arm listing gives the fall with age. The KUKA KR family has its own price level from its own listings, and each model above gets its own adjustment, pulled toward the family level when it has few listings. The band is where 8 in 10 asking prices would fall. The steps and the test results are on how we price robots.

What moves the price of one KUKA arm

The controller generation matters to buyers: a KR C4 cabinet is newer than a KR C2, and an arm sold without any controller is worth less to most buyers. So does the version: long-reach and shelf-mounted versions price differently from the standard arm. Ask for the build year from the plate, the hours and a video of the arm moving, and price an arm you cannot see working nearer the auction floor.

Questions people ask

How much is a used KUKA robot?

A 10 year old KUKA KR arm typically asks $14,700 for the arm alone, and most ask between $7,800 and $27,800.

Which KUKA robot holds its value best?

Among the common models, the KR 180 asks the most at 10 years old ($19,800). All KUKA models lose asking price at about the same yearly rate.

What does the number in a KUKA model name mean?

Roughly the weight the arm can carry, in kilograms: a KR 16 carries about 16 and a KR 210 about 210.

What is a used KUKA KR 210 worth?

A 10 year old KR 210 typically asks $13,800 for the arm alone. Our KR 210 guide goes through it age by age.

What would a used KUKA arm sell for?

We estimate a planned private sale of a 10 year old KUKA KR arm at about $6,100, and a rushed auction sale at about $2,600.

Where do these prices come from?

From public listings of used robot arms and matched auction results. The method is on how we price robots.
