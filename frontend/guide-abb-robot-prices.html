ABB prices by series and age

ABB names its arms IRB and a number. The IRB 6000 series (6400, 6600, 6640, 6650, 6700) are the big arms that carry well over 100 kilograms, used for spot welding, handling and palletizing. The rest run from the small IRB 120 and 140 up to the IRB 7600, one of the strongest arms ABB has made.

Reading an ABB model name

An ABB name such as IRB 6640-205/2.75 tells you two things after the model number: the load the arm carries in kilograms (205) and how far it reaches in metres (2.75). Versions of one model with a longer reach usually carry less. Our prices are set at the model level, so an IRB 6640 of any version reads the same figure, and a listing for an unusual version can sit well away from it. Check that the version on offer carries enough for your job before you compare prices.

ABB against KUKA and FANUC

At 10 years old, an IRB 6000 series arm asks $12,400, against $14,700 for a KUKA KR arm and $17,600 for a FANUC M-series arm. The big ABB arms ask a little less than the other two makers' families at the same age. The gap is smaller than the range around any one figure, so the model, its version and its condition matter more than the badge.

What sells and for how much

We have few matched auction sales of ABB arms, so for what an ABB arm would sell for we use the share measured across every arm: a middle 50% of the asking price at auction for arms under 10, and 17% for arms 10 or older. The expected sale sits halfway between that floor and the asking price on a ratio scale.

How we worked it out

ABB asking prices fall at the rate we measured across every arm, about 4.1% a year. Each series has its own price level and each model its own adjustment from its own listings, pulled toward its series when it has few. The steps are on how we price robots.

Before you buy a used ABB

Check which controller comes with the arm: IRC5 cabinets are the common one on arms of this age, and older S4C cabinets are harder to support. Ask for the hours, the service record and whether the arm has the right dress pack for your job: the hoses and cables that run along the arm to its tool are specific to welding, painting or handling, and replacing them adds cost. Ask too whether the arm has been rebuilt, and by whom. Price an arm you cannot see working nearer the auction floor.

Questions people ask

How much is a used ABB robot?

At 10 years old, an IRB 6000 series arm typically asks $12,400 and other IRB arms $14,900, for the arm alone.

What is a used ABB IRB 6640 worth?

A 10 year old IRB 6640 typically asks $11,500, most ask between $6,700 and $19,600, and a planned private sale would likely bring about $4,800.

Which ABB robot holds its value best?

In our data the mid-size IRB 1600 and IRB 2600 ask the most for their age. All ABB arms lose asking price at about the same yearly rate.

How fast do ABB robots lose value?

About 4.1% of their asking price a year, with a sharper drop in what they sell for after 10 years old.

What do the numbers after an ABB model name mean?

In a name such as IRB 6640-205/2.75, 205 is the load the arm carries in kilograms and 2.75 is its reach in metres.

Do these prices include the controller?

No. Our figures are for the arm alone, without the controller, the tool on its end or the work to set it up.
