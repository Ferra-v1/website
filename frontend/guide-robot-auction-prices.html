What do used industrial robots sell for at auction?

Used robot arms sold at auction for a middle 24% of what sellers ask for the same robot at the same age.

Age splits it sharply: arms under 10 years old brought a middle 50% of the asking price, and arms 10 or older 17%.

The spread is wide, from 15% to 72%. The three that sold near 70% were young R-2000iB arms from one sale, and the arms of 14 years and older all brought under a quarter of the asking price.

Why auctions pay so much less than asking prices

A listing can wait months for the right buyer, and its price is a starting point for haggling. An auction sells on the day to whoever is in the room, often dealers who will clean, test and relist the arm with a margin on top. Buyers at an auction also take the arm as it is, usually without a warranty and without seeing it run. All of that comes off the price.

Age widens the gap. Parts and support for old arms are harder to find, older controllers run software fewer integrators know, and buyers discount for the risk that a gearbox or motor will need work.

What this means for a price

We use the auction share as the floor of what a robot is worth: what a rushed sale would bring. For a typical 10 year old arm asking $14,700, the floor is $2,600. A planned private sale, where the owner has time to find a buyer, lands between the floor and the asking price. We put it halfway on a ratio scale, $6,100 for that arm. If you are selling, an auction is the fastest way out and the most expensive one. If you are buying, auctions are where the bargains are, as long as you can test the arm or price in the risk of repairs.

What to keep in mind

  • The auctions ran over several years and we do not adjust their prices for inflation, so older sales read a little low next to today's asking prices.
  • Some auction houses add a buyer's premium of about 10% to the hammer price and some do not. We use the price as the auction house reports it.
  • Whole welding cells, arms sold in pieces and robots we could not identify are left out, because they cannot be compared with a single arm.

Questions people ask

What do used robots sell for at auction?

A middle 24% of what sellers ask for the same robot at the same age, in the sales we matched.

Do younger robots sell for a bigger share at auction?

Yes. Arms under 10 years old brought a middle 50% of the asking price, and arms 10 or older 17%.

Is buying a robot at auction a good idea?

Prices are far below asking prices, but you usually take the arm as it is, without a warranty or a chance to see it run.

Where are industrial robots auctioned?

Online industrial auction houses that sell plant equipment when factories close or upgrade. The table names the ones in our data.

Do auction prices include the buyer's premium?

It depends on the auction house. We use the price as each house reports it, and say on our method page which add a premium.
