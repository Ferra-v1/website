Who gets to
own the robots?

Right now, most people have no way to own any of them.

We are starting by putting a fair price on every robot arm, then every robot, so people can trust what it is worth.

Example prices for whole new robots, fully set up. The box at the top prices a used arm without its setup.

Once people can trust the price, more of them can put their money behind robots. One day, we want anyone to be able to own a piece of the robots doing the work.

What if a loan goes bad?

A lender can sell the robots if a company fails. A forced sale may not cover the loan, so we show lenders the sums.

About 2 to 12 robots' worth could come back, against a loan worth 35 robots.

15 robots' worth is what the company paid.

Auctions can pay far less.

We found 10 auction sales of used arms. Sold at the middle price, all 50 would bring what 2 new robots cost as bare arms, or what 12 cost with everything fitted. We cannot yet say which is closer.

Where the numbers come from

  • Our example is 50 new robot arms, each fully installed with its gripper and controller, at $125,000 each, or $6,250,000 in all. A loan of 70% is $4,375,000, with a fee of about 1.5% ($65,625) when the loan starts, paid back in equal monthly parts over 36 months. For a used robot, our example lends 70% of our estimate of what it is worth, set up and working. Real terms depend on the lender and the robots. Nothing on this site is an offer or commitment to lend.
