Why we are building Ferra

Robot companies rent their robots out. Every robot they deploy is paid for up front, and raising equity for each one is slow and expensive.

Lenders already finance trucks, aircraft and factory machines, because they know what those machines resell for. Robots have lacked that price history.

Ferra is building it. We price robots from used-robot listings and auction results, and use those prices to arrange loans from lenders who finance equipment.

That is the destination: every robot carries a price, and a robot company finances its fleet the way a trucking company finances its trucks.

    Notes

    1. Example fleet: 50 industrial arms, each a complete palletizing cell (arm, gripper and controller, installed) at $125,000, a fleet value of $6,250,000. A 70% advance gives a $4,375,000 loan, with a fee of about 1.5% at close ($65,625). Charts show the loan repaid in equal monthly amounts of principal over 36 months, the term the price tool uses for this cell. Actual terms depend on the lender and the robots. Nothing on this site is an offer or commitment to lend.
    2. Resale values by year follow the risk engine's curve for a palletizing cell: 80% of the new installed price after one year, 60% after two, 44% after three. The same curve sets the expected loss figures and the price tool's values for robots up to 2 years old. Used-robot listings price the robot alone, so they sit lower. See the methodology.
